# Persona Synthesis

## Source coverage

- **Files analyzed:** `projects/starter/input/telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md`.
- **Files not analyzed:** None. The only supported source in the specified input folder was readable.
- **Personas produced:** 3 provisional end-user personas. The BRD's “Internal Product and Operations Team” is treated as a stakeholder group, not an end-user persona.
- **Evidence limitations:** The source is one business requirements document. It contains business-authored target-user definitions, example statements, scenarios, requirements, risks, and proposed metrics, but no research method, participant sample, transcripts, observations, survey results, analytics, dates, or independent corroboration. HIGH therefore means the BRD directly makes the cited claim; it does not mean the claim has been validated with users. Statements introduced as things users “often say” have no study provenance and should not be treated as verified quotations.

## Confidence legend

- **HIGH:** Directly supported by cited source evidence. In this deliverable, HIGH items are direct BRD claims rather than validated user-research findings unless stated otherwise.
- **MEDIUM:** A reasonable synthesis or inference with a stated, cited basis.
- **LOW:** An assumption or weakly supported interpretation requiring validation.
- **CONTRADICTORY:** Relevant sources conflict. No contradictions were found because only one source was supplied.

## Persona 1

### Name

Continuity-Focused Upgrader

### User type

- [HIGH] BRD-defined existing postpaid customer who wants to replace a current device while keeping the same phone number and wireless service. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “4.1 Existing Postpaid Customer”]

### Summary

- [HIGH] This customer needs quick confirmation of eligibility and remaining balance, compatible device choices, a straightforward order journey, and dependable activation on the existing line. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “5. User Needs — Existing Postpaid Customer Needs”]
- [MEDIUM] This persona is oriented toward service continuity and task completion more than exploratory shopping. Basis: the BRD emphasizes retaining the existing number and line, confirming eligibility, ordering an appropriate device, and completing activation with recovery support. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “1. Project Overview” and “5. User Needs — Existing Postpaid Customer Needs”]

### Goals

- [HIGH] Confirm whether the selected line is eligible and understand any remaining device balance or approved next step. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “5. User Needs — Existing Postpaid Customer Needs” and “7.1 Line Selection and Eligibility”]
- [HIGH] Replace the current device while keeping the same phone number and line. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “4.1 Existing Postpaid Customer” and “7.8 Activation on the Existing Line”]
- [HIGH] Choose a device that is compatible with the selected line, plan, network, SIM capability, and service location. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “7.2 Compatible Device Catalog”]
- [HIGH] Complete the order, track fulfillment, and activate the new device with clear instructions and recovery support. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “7.7 Order Confirmation and Tracking” and “7.8 Activation on the Existing Line”]

### Pain points

- [HIGH] Business claim: customers may not know whether a line is eligible for an upgrade or how much remains due on the current device. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “2. Business Problem”]
- [HIGH] Business claim: customers may be uncertain which devices work with their plan. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “2. Business Problem”]
- [HIGH] Business-identified risk: inventory changes or activation failures can interrupt the journey after device selection or delivery. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “13. Risks — Risk 5”]

### Motivations

- [HIGH] Preserve the existing phone number and wireless line while upgrading only the device. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “2. Business Problem” and “4.1 Existing Postpaid Customer”]
- [HIGH] Complete the upgrade digitally with clear support when the journey cannot continue online. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “1. Project Overview” and “5. User Needs — Existing Postpaid Customer Needs”]

### Evidence

- [HIGH] BRD target-user claim: an active postpaid customer wants to replace the current device without changing number or service. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “4.1 Existing Postpaid Customer”]
- [HIGH] BRD user-needs claim: this group needs eligibility, balance, compatibility, checkout, tracking, activation, and fallback support. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “5. User Needs — Existing Postpaid Customer Needs”]
- [HIGH] Business-authored scenarios describe both an eligible financed upgrade and an upgrade requiring resolution of an ₹8,000 remaining balance. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “8. Example Scenarios — Scenario 1” and “8. Example Scenarios — Scenario 2”]

### Assumptions

- [LOW] Assumption: most continuity-focused customers prefer self-service through the complete activation step. Basis: the BRD aims to increase digital completion but also requires assisted recovery; it provides no channel-preference research. Validate by studying where customers switch to support and why. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “2. Business Problem,” “3. Product Goal,” and “14. Open Questions”]

## Persona 2

### Name

Price-Certainty Upgrader

### User type

- [HIGH] BRD-defined budget-conscious customer who wants to understand the device price, installment amount, taxes, promotions, trade-in credit, and total due before ordering. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “4.2 Budget-Conscious Customer”]

### Summary

- [HIGH] This customer needs a complete monthly and one-time price breakdown, comparison of eligible payment options, clear trade-in conditions, and confirmation of plan or bill impact. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “5. User Needs — Budget-Conscious Customer Needs”]
- [MEDIUM] This persona evaluates an upgrade through total financial impact rather than the advertised device price alone. Basis: the BRD combines retail price, installments, taxes, fees, promotions, credits, remaining balance, trade-in, add-ons, and expected bill impact in the required disclosure. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “7.4 Transparent Pricing and Promotions”]

### Goals

- [HIGH] Understand the complete monthly and one-time cost before submitting an order. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “5. User Needs — Budget-Conscious Customer Needs” and “7.4 Transparent Pricing and Promotions”]
- [HIGH] Compare eligible financing, full retail payment, and any approved down-payment combination. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “5. User Needs — Budget-Conscious Customer Needs” and “7.6 Payment and Fulfillment”]
- [HIGH] Understand promotion details, expected credits, and the effect on the monthly bill. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “7.4 Transparent Pricing and Promotions”]
- [HIGH] Review a conditional trade-in estimate, its requirements, deadline, inspection process, and reasons the final value may change. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “7.5 Trade-In”]

### Pain points

- [HIGH] Business claim: the monthly price can appear to change by checkout. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “2. Business Problem”]
- [HIGH] Business claim: customers may not understand how trade-in value is calculated. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “2. Business Problem”]
- [HIGH] Business-identified risk: eligibility, balance, price, promotion, or tax data may be outdated unless authoritative systems are used and values are revalidated. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “13. Risks — Risk 1”]

### Motivations

- [HIGH] Avoid unexpected charges during checkout. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “5. User Needs — Budget-Conscious Customer Needs”]
- [HIGH] Make an informed purchase decision using the full amount due today and expected ongoing bill impact. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “4.2 Budget-Conscious Customer” and “7.4 Transparent Pricing and Promotions”]

### Evidence

- [HIGH] BRD target-user claim: the budget-conscious customer needs visibility into device price, installments, taxes, promotions, trade-in credit, and total due. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “4.2 Budget-Conscious Customer”]
- [HIGH] BRD user-needs claim: this group needs comprehensive price transparency, payment comparison, trade-in clarity, and confirmation of plan or bill impact. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “5. User Needs — Budget-Conscious Customer Needs”]
- [HIGH] Business-authored scenario: a trade-in customer is shown a ₹12,000 estimate explicitly subject to inspection and receives return instructions after accepting terms. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “8. Example Scenarios — Scenario 3”]

### Assumptions

- [LOW] Assumption: presenting all cost components together will be sufficient for customers to understand the final financial commitment. Basis: the BRD requires complete disclosure but provides no comprehension testing or evidence about information density. Validate with task-based usability and price-recall testing across mobile and web. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “7.4 Transparent Pricing and Promotions” and “11. Non-Functional Requirements — Mobile Usability”]

## Persona 3

### Name

Guidance-Seeking Device Chooser

### User type

- [HIGH] BRD-defined customer who is unfamiliar with device specifications and wants simple recommendations based on budget and preferences. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “4.3 Customer Needing Device Guidance”]

### Summary

- [HIGH] This customer needs simple preference questions, a small set of compatible and available recommendations, plain-language reasons, easy feature comparison, and freedom to browse without recommendations. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “5. User Needs — Customer Needing Device Guidance Needs”]
- [MEDIUM] This persona needs decision support that reduces comparison effort without taking control away. Basis: the BRD cites difficulty choosing without comparing every model and requires optional, explainable recommendations that can be ignored. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “2. Business Problem,” “5. User Needs — Customer Needing Device Guidance Needs,” and “7.3 AI-Assisted Device Recommendations”]

### Goals

- [HIGH] Find suitable devices using simple questions about budget, camera, battery, performance, screen size, operating system, and storage. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “7.3 AI-Assisted Device Recommendations”]
- [HIGH] Receive recommendations restricted to compatible, available, and commercially eligible devices. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “5. User Needs — Customer Needing Device Guidance Needs” and “7.3 AI-Assisted Device Recommendations”]
- [HIGH] Understand in plain language why each device is recommended. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “5. User Needs — Customer Needing Device Guidance Needs” and “7.3 AI-Assisted Device Recommendations”]
- [HIGH] Compare devices or bypass recommendations and browse the full eligible catalog. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “5. User Needs — Customer Needing Device Guidance Needs”]

### Pain points

- [HIGH] Business claim: some customers need help choosing a phone without comparing every model. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “2. Business Problem”]
- [HIGH] BRD user-needs claim: technical jargon and overconfident recommendation language are inappropriate for this group. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “5. User Needs — Customer Needing Device Guidance Needs”]
- [HIGH] Business-identified risk: an AI recommendation may not meet the customer's needs. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “13. Risks — Risk 2”]

### Motivations

- [HIGH] Narrow device choices using stated budget and feature preferences. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “4.3 Customer Needing Device Guidance” and “7.3 AI-Assisted Device Recommendations”]
- [HIGH] Make a device decision with understandable reasons while retaining the ability to choose independently. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “5. User Needs — Customer Needing Device Guidance Needs” and “7.3 AI-Assisted Device Recommendations”]

### Evidence

- [HIGH] BRD target-user claim: this group lacks familiarity with specifications and wants simple budget- and preference-based recommendations. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “4.3 Customer Needing Device Guidance”]
- [HIGH] BRD user-needs claim: recommendation controls must include compatibility, availability, explanations, comparison, bypass, and jargon avoidance. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “5. User Needs — Customer Needing Device Guidance Needs”]
- [HIGH] Business-authored scenario: when the recommendation service is unavailable, the customer can browse compatible devices and complete the upgrade without AI. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, section “8. Example Scenarios — Scenario 4”]

### Assumptions

- [LOW] Assumption: answering preference questions reduces effort enough to justify an AI-assisted step. Basis: the BRD proposes optional questions and recommendations but provides no evidence about completion time, recommendation acceptance, or perceived usefulness. Validate by comparing assisted and standard catalog journeys. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “7.3 AI-Assisted Device Recommendations” and “12. Success Metrics”]

## Cross-persona design considerations

- [HIGH] The journey must show only lines customers are authorized to manage and only devices compatible and commercially eligible for the selected line. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “10. Functional Requirements — FR1” and “10. Functional Requirements — FR3”]
- [HIGH] Eligibility, price, promotions, inventory, payment, identity, and fulfillment must be revalidated before order confirmation. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “7.6 Payment and Fulfillment” and “10. Functional Requirements — FR9”]
- [HIGH] Repeated taps or retries must not create duplicate orders, payments, financing agreements, trade-ins, or activations. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “7.7 Order Confirmation and Tracking” and “11. Non-Functional Requirements — Reliability”]
- [HIGH] AI may recommend and explain devices, but approved systems—not AI—must determine eligibility, credit, fraud, prices, promotions, financing, and order acceptance. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “6. Proposed Solution” and “7.4 Transparent Pricing and Promotions”]
- [HIGH] The app and website must meet WCAG 2.2 Level AA and provide understandable errors, clear mobile progress, readable pricing, and recovery paths. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “11. Non-Functional Requirements — Accessibility” and “11. Non-Functional Requirements — Mobile Usability”]
- [MEDIUM] The design should reveal eligibility and cost constraints before asking customers to invest heavily in device comparison. Basis: the BRD places line selection and eligibility before the catalog and identifies unclear eligibility and changing prices as core problems. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “2. Business Problem,” “7.1 Line Selection and Eligibility,” and “7.2 Compatible Device Catalog”]

### Evidence gaps and weaknesses

- All personas come from one BRD, so repetition across target users, user needs, scenarios, requirements, risks, and acceptance criteria is not independent corroboration.
- No participant count, recruitment criteria, raw research notes, survey data, behavioural analytics, support-contact analysis, or source dates are supplied.
- The BRD's user quotations have no participant or study provenance and are usable only as business claims about user sentiment.
- The personas are likely to overlap: any existing postpaid customer may also be budget-conscious or need device guidance. The BRD does not show whether these are stable segments, temporary needs, or journey states.
- The BRD does not establish the prevalence or relative priority of the three user types.
- Proposed improvements and percentages are success targets, not observed outcomes.

### Contradictions requiring review

- None surfaced within the single supplied source. Absence of contradiction should not be interpreted as corroboration.

### Hypotheses and assumptions to test

- Determine where customers abandon self-service and which eligibility, balance, fulfillment, or activation issues trigger assisted support.
- Test whether the full pricing disclosure is understandable and memorable without overwhelming customers, especially on small screens.
- Research how customers interpret conditional trade-in estimates and value-adjustment notices.
- Compare AI-assisted and standard catalog journeys for decision time, confidence, suitability, completion, and recommendation bypass.
- Validate whether the three BRD-defined groups represent distinct behavioural patterns or overlapping needs that should instead shape one adaptive journey.
- Test privacy expectations and comprehension of how account data and stated preferences are used in recommendations.

### Persona relationship

- These are provisional, overlapping need-based archetypes rather than mutually exclusive customer segments. An existing postpaid customer may simultaneously prioritize service continuity, price certainty, and device-selection guidance. The single BRD does not establish stable segment membership or prevalence. [Source: telecom-device-upgrade-existing-line-brd.md, sections “4. Target Users” and “5. User Needs”]

### Review guidance

- **HIGH:** May inform current design as a direct representation of the BRD, while preserving its single-source and business-authored limitations. Do not present HIGH items as validated user behaviour without further research.
- **MEDIUM:** Treat as a design hypothesis and test before relying on it for consequential decisions.
- **LOW:** Validate through research before using it to determine product direction.
- **CONTRADICTORY:** Do not use for irreversible decisions until the conflict is investigated. No contradictory insights were found in this single-source analysis.
